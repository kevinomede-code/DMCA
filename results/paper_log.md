**Lu et al. 2020 (already in graph)** — Section VII of this Community 8 hub paper explicitly flags "multi-model catalog drift response" as an open research direction. This is the strongest possible citation for DMCA's novelty claim: the thesis is a direct instantiation of a gap flagged but not pursued by Lu et al. Cite this in the introduction as "Lu et al. (2020) identify X as an open problem; this thesis proposes Y as a solution."

**Shankar et al. 2022** — Practitioner evidence (18 interviews at major tech/industrial companies) confirming that drift-triggered model re-selection has zero tooling support. This moves DMCA's motivation from theoretical to empirically validated — use in the introduction and in GAP 1 justification.

**CACE principle (Sculley 2015)** — DMCA's model-swap approach is architecturally safer than MLOps retraining under CACE: by selecting a new pre-trained model rather than retraining the existing one, the feature pipeline and calibration chain remain stable. This is a concrete technical advantage of DMCA over retrain-based MLOps that can be quantified in the experimental evaluation (e.g., number of pipeline components requiring re-validation after drift response).
