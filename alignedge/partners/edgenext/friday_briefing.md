**Date:** Friday (post pitch)
**Context:** EdgeNext (Beijing Founder Program partner) asked for deck. Interested in collaborating to productize AlignEdge and sell to their client base.
**Stated angle:** "collaborate to make AlignEdge a product, they think it can improve a lot, sell to their clients"

## 4 possible deal structures (identify which they're proposing)

The phrase "collaborate to make it a product and sell to our clients" is ambiguous. Could mean 4 very different relationships:

### A · Channel Partner (non-exclusive) — LOW RISK for AlignEdge
- They resell AlignEdge audits to their MaaS customer base
- You keep IP, keep pricing power, keep other verticals
- Revenue share: 15-25% to EdgeNext for referrals
- Recommended structure

### B · Strategic Partner (co-development) — MEDIUM RISK
- They contribute engineering resources to productize
- Deeper technical integration with their stack
- Revenue share: 30-50% + possibly equity
- Risk: operational entanglement, IP capture

### C · Anchor Customer — MEDIUM-HIGH RISK
- They are the first big customer, define spec
- You build what serves them specifically
- Multi-year contract but you serve their spec
- Risk: customer captivity, less generalizable product

### D · Acquihire in slow motion — HIGH RISK
- Start collaboration, build dependency
- They acquire you in 12-18 months at depressed valuation
- Risk: loss of optionality entirely

## 4 questions to identify the scenario (ask early)

Ask in first 10-15 minutes:

1. *"Would this be exclusive distribution to your customer base, or could I keep selling directly to other verticals like automotive tier-one?"*
   → Exclusive/hesitation = Scenario C or D

2. *"Would you contribute engineering resources to the product build, and if so, how would IP ownership work?"*
   → Yes + joint IP = Scenario B

3. *"Are you thinking of this as a commercial partnership, or is there a strategic investment discussion?"*
   → Strategic investment mentioned = Scenario D possible

4. *"What's your ideal timeline for a formal agreement structure?"*
   → "Move fast, weeks" = B/C
   → "Explore over months" = A comfort zone

## Additional discovery questions (once scenario identified)

- *"What specifically in the deck caught your attention?"* → identifies pain area
- *"Are you seeing this pain internally, or with your MaaS customers?"* → discriminates internal use vs channel
- *"How do your customers currently validate models before deploying to your infrastructure?"* → identifies current gap
- *"Who else in your organization is looking at this problem?"* → identifies tech vs business side conversation
- *"What does 'improve a lot' mean specifically? Which parts?"* → gets concrete product feedback

## What YOU want from EdgeNext

Don't go into meeting only defensively. Have your asks ready:

### Minimum win outcomes (get at least 2 of 3):
1. **Design partner status** — even informal email confirmation. They as first customer (paid or unpaid).
2. **Warm intro to 1-2 of their customers** — anyone from Microsoft/Sony/Audi/etc. that has edge AI pain.
3. **Follow-up meeting confirmed with timeline** — 2-4 weeks out, specific agenda ("draft partnership term sheet" or "technical POC review").

### If A (Channel Partner) emerges:
- Push for **non-exclusive** contract
- 15-20% revenue share (not 25%+)
- Right to sell in EU/US/other verticals unrestricted
- Case study rights for AlignEdge marketing

### If B/C (deeper structures) emerge:
- Say: "Let me finish my next 90 days (design partners, price validation) before we structure something deeper. We can revisit with more data on both sides."
- This buys optionality without rejecting them

## What NOT to sign Friday (non-negotiable)

- ❌ Exclusivity clauses ("only sell to us or through us")
- ❌ Joint IP creation ("what we build together is co-owned")
- ❌ Non-compete on Chinese cloud edge (Alibaba, Tencent restriction)
- ❌ Right of first refusal on future financing
- ❌ Customer priority requirement (their customers > others)
- ❌ Revenue share > 30% without meaningful equity in exchange
- ❌ Timeline pressure ("sign by end of week")

**Standard line for any of these:**
> *"I need my legal review before signing this. Standard process. Send it to me, I'll come back within 10 business days with markup."*

## Framing psicologico

They are big, you are small. But **they made the first move** (asking for deck). That's leverage narrative you keep.

Don't go in "thank you for meeting". Go in "let's explore reciprocal fit".

If pressure builds toward accepting scenario B/C too fast:

> *"I appreciate your interest. Let me be clear about my position — I have three other design partner conversations progressing in parallel, and I'm calibrating structure based on which relationship gives me the best combination of validation, distribution, and independence. I want us to be a strong fit — but on terms that let me build a real company, not just a channel product."*

Not aggressive, is clear. Communicates: you have optionality, know your value, negotiate from mutual respect.

## Post-meeting protocol

Within 24 hours after meeting:

1. **30 minutes alone in a coffee shop** — process, don't respond to emails yet
2. Ask yourself the KEY question:
   > "Does this collaboration make ME more valuable as a founder, or does it make me EdgeNext's strategic employee?"
3. If answer is "more valuable" → proceed with structure discussion
4. If answer is "strategic employee" → renegotiate or decline politely
5. Follow-up email within 48h with confirmed next step
